The delivery loop's stages exist and its TRANSITIONS do not — 8 of 16 stages are mechanical and the rest happen because a seat remembers, which is why two of the operator's four standing asks have survived three sessions each. **The conductor is a state machine over rows:** intake ACCEPTED → row filed; row + contract → dispatched; merged → docs rendered, telemetry written, closure proposed; ratified → row archived. **The operator's five decisions are the only manual transitions** — this automates transitions that are already mechanical and moves no decision away from him. Two standing asks land here and nowhere else: *tasks at open, archived at close* (the archiver has no trigger) and *telemetry used* (emitted for three sessions, consumed by nothing) — the "merged → telemetry written" leg is what makes the second one true. **Seam with `[#664]`, stated so neither row absorbs the other:** `propose_closures` writing one file per run, and `archive_row_body` GAINING its trigger, are `writes` / `triggers` edges of the spine arc and are explicitly **not** separate rows (DECLARE-SPINE § 4); the conductor CONSUMES those edges to decide a transition.
