- [#768] [P1][M] **A gate has one refusal channel, so a violation and an environment it could not evaluate are indistinguishable to the committer** -- AJ M04 L02 draws exactly this distinction and makes it structural: a protocol-level error is CRITICAL and must never carry an application-level condition -- *"jeżeli np. narzędzie nie znajdzie jakiegoś użytkownika w bazie, to nie powinniście zwracać błędu na poziomie protokołu JSON-RPC, bo błąd protokołu jako taki jest błędem krytycznym"* (`AJ_M04_transkrypcje.md:195-197`) -- and the application-level condition comes back on the SUCCESS path instead: *"Zamiast tego powinien się zwrócić normalny wynik, natomiast pole isError ustawić na true"* (`198-199`). Both verified. Two channels, deliberately: *the call worked and the answer is no* is not *the call was malformed*. · **THIS LANE IS THE WITNESS, measured rather than argued.** `decision-coverage` refused this lane's first commit naming four transport files the operator dropped into `$CLAUDE_PROMPTS_DIR/to-cc/` between 00:24 and 00:40 that night, all four carrying `carried-by: docs/audits/2026-09-15-technical-batch-z-manifest.md` -- a path only the integrator can land on `main`. `scripts/decision_coverage.py check` refuses IDENTICALLY with an empty staged set, so the refusal was a property of the tree and the transport and had nothing to do with the diff. Nothing in the repo had changed: main's own last commit at the time predated the drop and had committed clean. · **Every LOCAL lane in the batch hit the same wall simultaneously**, and the night plan's own precondition -- *"every lane's first commit files or amends its own row"* -- was unsatisfiable for all of them until an unrelated seat landed the manifest. · **The repo already reasons about posture, and that is the point rather than a counter-argument.** `.claude/settings.json` argues fail-CLOSED for the ADR-77 transcript guard and fail-OPEN for `[#727]`'s deny-and-point guard, each with its measured reason. That reasoning is per-hook, hand-written, in prose, and lives in a file the refusing hook never reads. The REFUSAL ITSELF carries no channel for *"this is not yours"*, so a lane cannot tell a gate that caught it from a gate that could not evaluate, and the only levers are a sanctioned `SKIP=` or a wait. · **Why P1.** The failure is not a wrong verdict, it is an unattributable one, and unattributable refusals are what make a `--no-verify` reflex reasonable-looking -- the exact reflex this repo bans without exception. · Done when: a commit-tier refusal carries a machine-readable DISPOSITION distinguishing a violation the committer introduced from a precondition the committer cannot satisfy, the second class names the surface and the seat that CAN satisfy it, and at least one existing gate -- `decision-coverage` is the measured instance -- is converted to emit it, so that a lane meeting the second class is told what it is waiting for rather than told it failed · refs `AJ_M04_transkrypcje.md:195-199` (the two-channel rule, quoted above), `docs/audits/2026-09-15-technical-lane-z-10-aj-m04-evidence/LOCATOR-VERIFICATION.md` (items 09, 10 -- verified), `scripts/decision_coverage.py` (the measured instance), `.claude/settings.json` (the per-hook posture reasoning, in prose), `[#764]` (the row whose landing cleared the block, proving the committer was never the party who could), `[#727]` (the fail-OPEN guard, the deliberate opposite posture) · kill-candidates: none -- `[#764]` discharges the ONE occurrence by supplying the missing row and leaves the channel defect untouched, which is why the block recurred for every lane before it landed; `[#727]` owns one guard's posture rather than the refusal contract every gate shares · source: batch Z lane z-10, AJ M04 first pass, `[#765]`
