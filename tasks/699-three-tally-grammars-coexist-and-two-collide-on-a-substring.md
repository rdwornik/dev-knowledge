- [#699] [P2][S] **Three tally grammars coexist and two collide on a substring, so which one a first-match reader resolves is decided by ordering rather than by meaning** - Second of the three rows the batch X roster section 4 bundles as the SEAT-BOOT cluster, carried at `docs/intake/2026-09-11-tech-batch-x-roster.md` (intake 93). AW5-1 names it as the defect filed "beside" the per-merge-GO one, and AW5-4 names the reader: a line consumed first-match at `scripts/seat_refusals.py:558`. · **First-substring-match ordering is load-bearing, and that is the defect rather than a symptom of it.** Where three grammars can describe a tally and two of them share a substring, the grammar a reader resolves to is whichever pattern it happens to try first. That makes the ORDER of the patterns a silent semantic decision: reorder them during an unrelated refactor and tallies start meaning something different, with no test failing and no diff that looks like a behaviour change. · **The failure is quiet in the direction that matters.** A tally misread as a different grammar does not raise; it yields a number, and the number gates a merge. A collision that resolved the wrong way looks exactly like a review that passed. · **`[#649]` is adjacent and does NOT absorb this row.** `[#649]` owns the CONTENT of the tally line -- that `HIGH = 0` must be `HIGH raw=N fixed=N unresolved=0` and a single-number tally is `review=NONE` -- and its Done-when has the templates emit the three-part line and the integrator check read the same predicate. Adopting one canonical line does not retire the two legacy grammars already in the corpus, and a reader that still disambiguates by first-match will still mis-resolve them. The two rows want the same end state and are reached from opposite ends; they may merge at grooming. · Done when: the live tally grammars are enumerated with the colliding substring named; the reader no longer depends on first-match ordering to disambiguate them -- either one grammar survives and the others are REFUSED, or the read is anchored so a collision cannot resolve silently; and one test FAILS when a tally written in one grammar is read as another · **Done-when authored by the filing lane, not carried**: the roster names this item in a section-4 prose list that carries no Done-when column · refs `docs/intake/2026-09-11-tech-batch-x-roster.md` (intake 93, section 4), `to-cc/AMEND-BATCH-W-005.md` AW5-1 (filed beside the GO defect) and AW5-4 (`Tally:` read first-match at `seat_refusals.py:558`), `scripts/seat_refusals.py`, `templates/handoff/seats/`, `[#649]` (the tally's CONTENT -- adjacent, not absorbing), `[#671]` (a lane branch carrying no tally at all), `[#698]` and `[#700]` (the other two rows of this cluster) · kill-candidates: none -- `[#649]` is named above as adjacent-not-absorbing and stays open on its own merits; `[#671]` owns the ABSENT tally, which is the case where no grammar is matched at all · source: `to-cc/DECLARE-BATCH-X-ROSTER-2026-09-11.md` section 4 with `to-cc/AMEND-BATCH-W-005.md` AW5-1, filed 2026-09-11 by lane `lane-x-000-batch-x-roster-lands`
