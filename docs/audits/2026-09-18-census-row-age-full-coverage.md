## 1 · Verdict

**"The backlog is an age problem" is REFUTED for the open set. The measured pressure is inflow.**

- **All 316 open rows** were covered, each one individually (appendix). The order's "267 remaining of
  317" is this same open set, 316 at `87638c8d`; ac-589's two reserved rows land at the merge.
- **Median open-row age: 17 days.** **111 open rows (35 %) were filed in the last 7 days**, and
  **213 (67 %) in the last 30.** Only **7 open rows are older than 90 days.**
- **1 abandonment candidate** (`[#342]`) and **29 WATCH rows** (age ≥45 d, idle ≥30 d). The 52
  DEFERRED rows are kept by class: each is pegged to a named trigger and not proposed.

The earlier "no row over 52 days" floor was an artifact, as ac-694 suspected. The `tasks/` tree begins
2026-07-27, so its own history dates every migrated row to that week. **Dated from BACKLOG.md history
instead, the oldest open row goes back to 2026-06-01.** That is a second floor: `[#id]` numbering starts
at the 2026-06-01 backlog migration, so any age before it cannot be recovered by id. It affects 4 rows.

## 2 · Method

**Organs first.** The row set and status come from `gen_task_tree.frontmatter_id` /
`frontmatter_status` over `tasks/*.md`, the queue organ's own parsers. Who cites a row comes from
FPG-1, `graph_store.ensure(root).in_edges("task:N")`. **One raw leg, declared:** git history, because
no organ holds a row's filing date.

| Field | Source |
|---|---|
| filed | earliest of: a `+` line in BACKLOG.md history carrying the id · the add of `tasks/N-*.md` · the first commit message citing `[#N]`. Resolved from BACKLOG history for 365 of 375 live rows, commit messages 9, tasks add 1 |
| last commit cite | newest commit message citing `[#N]`, **excluding sweep commits** that cite >10 ids |
| last audit cite | newest dated audit with an FPG-1 edge to `task:N`, **excluding sweep audits** that cite >25 rows (42 excluded) |
| idle | days since max(filed, last commit cite, last audit cite) |

**Why the sweep exclusion is load-bearing.** Grooming sheets, census appendices and closure-proposal
audits cite every open row. Counted as activity, they reset every row's idle clock at once: before the
exclusion, 2 of 375 live rows were idle more than 30 days, and after it, 31 open rows are. A sweep is evidence that a row
was *listed*, not that anyone *worked* it.

**Proposal rule.** OPEN, age ≥60 d and idle ≥45 d → ABANDONMENT-CANDIDATE. OPEN, age ≥45 d and
idle ≥30 d → WATCH. DEFERRED → KEEP. Anything else → ACTIVE.

## 5 · Anti-claims

- Filing dates before 2026-06-01 cannot be recovered by id (4 rows sit on that floor). Their true age
  is ≥ the reported age.
- FPG-1's inbound edges to a task node are **audit citations only** (consumer-at-landing). No edge kind
  records a live doc, script or config citing a row, so "still referenced live" could not be measured
  by the organ and was **dropped as a criterion** rather than approximated by grep.
- The sweep thresholds (10 ids per commit, 25 rows per audit) are judgment. Moving them changes the
  WATCH count at the margin, not the verdict: the age distribution does not depend on them.
- A bare `[#N]` in an old audit can name a different namespace's N. Three rows (`[#701]` `[#702]`
  `[#706]`) had audit citers that predate their filing, so activity is floored at the filing date.
