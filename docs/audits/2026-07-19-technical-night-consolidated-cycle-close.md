## 0. The operator's question, answered in one paragraph

**How the lifecycle works today vs how it should:** the fleet has strong **growth-side** enforcement — the tree is hermetically sealed against bad additions. E1 **fire-proved two organs this run** (the two-tier `validate_hermetization` gate + `fleet_parity` MUST-uniformity, both fire on a seeded divergence); the other growth-side guards (`validate_backlog`, `block-ff-push`, floor-hash) are **present and established but were not fire-tested this run** — asserted-sound, not proven-sound here (floor-hash parity is corroborated by S5's hash-match, not by a fire test). All three repos are **at floor-parity** (S5: consumers exact-match the hub floor hash + the 4 ARC-4 MUST-uniform values). But the fleet is **weakly instrumented on the lifecycle/decay side**: decisions land in ADR/BACKLOG but do **not** reliably propagate to (a) durable operational canon, (b) enforcement mechanisms, or (c) legibility surfaces. The single recurring failure mode across all nine streams is **"decision recorded ≠ decision enforced ≠ decision legible."** Four 2026-07-18/19 rulings are recorded but un-inoculated (S4); the intake→ADR decomposition and ADR intake-citation are unenforced (S1); there is no sanctioned deletion path, so the backlog accretes ~3.8 tasks/day against a groom scheduled 82 days out (S2); frozen ADR statuses and intake enum-drift have no reconciliation check (S3); five parallel-session incidents recovered after-the-fact with zero preventive gate (S6); no inbound-prompt completeness check exists (S7); the dynamic-testing capability exists but is gated by nothing (S9). Fleet-state does **not** need SQL/SIEM at current scale — the intake packs already ruled that; the real gap is a handful of missing single-purpose reporters (S8).

## 2. Cross-cutting meta-findings (the heart of "works vs should")

1. **The dominant failure mode is "recorded ≠ enforced ≠ legible."** A decision lands as an ADR/BACKLOG record (step 1) but does not propagate to durable operational canon (S4), to an enforcement mechanism (S1 ADR-citation, S2 deletion, S3 #242, S6 #353, S9 dynamic-gate), or to a legibility surface (S1/S3). Every stream is an instance of this one pattern. **This is the audit's headline.**
2. **Enforcement is asymmetric: strong on growth, weak on lifecycle.** The tree is hermetically sealed against bad *additions* (E1 proves the two-tier gate and fleet_parity fire) but has almost no gates for *decay/legibility*: no deletion path, no grooming cadence, no boot-contract, no dynamic-test evidence, no inbound-prompt check, no status-reconciliation. Well-sealed against bad growth; poorly instrumented against rot.
3. **"Legibility without motion" is aspirational, not built.** The stay-in-place convention (ruled) delivers immutability but the status/index surfaces that would make lifecycle state legible without moving files are largely unbuilt (enum drift, frozen ADR headers, no per-bundle status, no superseded-by field).
4. **Accretion is outpacing closure and the junkyard is structural.** ~3.8 net tasks/day, groom 82 days out, and no sanctioned deletion mechanism (#347 unruled) — the operator's named "junkyard/tombstone" pain has no organ.
5. **Consumers are AT floor-parity but the manifest under-declares reality.** The parity system works for what it tracks; the divergences it doesn't yet track (backlog schema, the `validate_backlog` fork) are exactly where the next equalization legs live.
6. **Fleet-state does not need SQL/SIEM.** The one stream that could have justified a big build (S8) instead found the intake packs already ruled it out and the honest gap is two small reporters. Correctly *not* over-building.
